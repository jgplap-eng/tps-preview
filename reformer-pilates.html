What it is

Strength, control and length on the carriage

The reformer is a sliding carriage attached to springs. Push or pull against them and you train the whole body under constant, adjustable resistance. Lighter springs assist a beginner through a movement; heavier springs load it for an experienced client. Because the carriage moves, every exercise also demands control, which is where the deep core does its work.

At Tessavi, classes follow the STOTT PILATES® approach: contemporary, anatomy-based, and focused on neutral alignment and breath. Expect footwork, bridging, leg and arm spring series, planks on the carriage and, at higher levels, inversions and standing work.

Core and postureDeep abdominal and back strength that carries into how you sit, stand and lift.
Joint-friendly resistanceSpring load is smooth and scalable, kinder on knees and backs than free weights.
Full-body, low impactLegs, glutes, arms and back in one session, with no jumping unless you want it.

Who it is for

From first-timers to athletes

Most clients arrive with one of four goals: a stronger core and better posture after long days at a desk or behind the wheel; a low-impact way to get fit again; cross-training for running, golf, diving or the gym; or a more precise, mindful practice than a crowded class can offer. The reformer handles all four because the load is adjustable and the coaching is close.

Men train here too, and so do clients in their fifties and sixties. If you are managing an injury, pregnancy or a medical condition, message us first so we can recommend a private session and the right instructor.

FAQ

Reformer Pilates, explained

What is Reformer Pilates?

Reformer Pilates is Pilates performed on a sliding-carriage machine with adjustable springs, straps and a footbar. The springs add resistance or assistance, so the same exercise can be made gentler for a beginner or harder for an athlete. Sessions build core strength, mobility, posture and control.

Is Reformer Pilates good for beginners?

Yes. The reformer supports your body through each movement, which makes it easier to learn alignment than on the mat. Start with our Foundation Group Reformer class or a private session, where the instructor sets up the machine for you.

Reformer or mat: which should I choose?

Reformer classes use spring resistance and give more feedback and variety; mat classes rely on body weight and small props and are excellent for precision and control. Many clients combine both. If you are new, begin on the reformer in a Foundation class.

How often should I do Reformer Pilates?

Two to three sessions a week gives steady progress. Our 6, 12 and 24-session packages are valid for one, two and three months, which lines up with that rhythm.

Will I be sore after my first class?

Usually a little, mostly in the deep core and inner thighs. It settles within a day or two and eases as your body adapts.

What should I wear and bring?

Fitted activewear you can move in, and grip socks (available at the studio). Bring water. Arrive about ten minutes early for your first visit.
